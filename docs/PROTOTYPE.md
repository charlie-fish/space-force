## Playable scope

Two to seven people share one browser/device. Normal turns, selectable ten-Tech displays, all 30 Techs and all 30 Events with sealed bidding form a complete local game. Last ten Techs and Event Batch 1 are the defaults. Every new game draws ten Events for two/three players (six rounds) or eight for four to seven (five rounds), including the ten-card test pools. Ragnarök can still shorten play. Three disjoint ten-card Event pools cover all 30 cards, with the same player-count limit as Standard random decks. Simpler Events and the labelled legacy Foresight test remain available. Foresight is legal with full Events; simpler Events exclude it and use R08 replacements. All printed Tech presets, playtest displays, Random and the R01 starting-Tech scenario remain. Seat order is entered at setup; the first seat starts. A map code seeds setup and hidden decks reproducibly. See [the 0.20 guide](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.20.md) for all batch cards, controls and Event conventions.

Each player chooses a home by button or double-click/double-tap, receiving three Settlements and three Ships. The optional R01 designer scenario starts every player with the five chain Techs, accounting for their markers from Reserve; it is saved/exported as a setup variable and scores those Techs normally. This permits immediate testing at all player counts, since four-plus-player normal games cannot acquire all five before their final ACTIONS phase. PLAN places four chips each in order, with a changeable preview followed by “Place chip here” or double-click/double-tap confirmation. ACTIONS skips players with no available top chip; each player resolves their four chips into unused action slots, acting or Investing. Economy cashes Investments on the Board, discards the previous Event, reveals a pair for sealed bidding and winner selection, resolves immediate Event choices, permits one Tech purchase each, reinforces on the Board and automatically passes first player. Simpler Events keep the review/continue step. Standard two/three-player games have six rounds; four to seven have five. Test batches now follow the same counts; Ragnarök can shorten play. Politics leaves three PLAN tokens and one blocked slot; Cyber Attack permits borrowed top tokens; Flexibility separates performed action and slot. The Economy after the last pair has been consumed still permits Tech purchases, then scores without reinforcement.

All ten Family Techs work: Agility, Artificial Warp, Assembly Line, Banking, Disruptor, Genesis, Kessler Syndrome, Patents, Planet Killer, and Planet Nine. Optional acquisition placements can be declined; Genesis and Disruptor resolve their required legal choices. The five-player setup consumes the spare asteroid token. Explore-token black holes have a separate supply from Planet Killer's feature token. An area enclosed by asteroid belts is legal if warps still connect it.

Ten more Techs now work: Population Explosion, Government Incentives, Frontier Pioneer, Rapid Advance, Spice, Arcologies, Cloaking, Patrol, Research Lab and Strategic Stall. Free actions resolve nested before/body/after effects while retaining the root turn, token and slot. Rapid Advance follows the almanac and is not SETTLE; Spice cannot trigger itself and recomputes its second output after the first chain. Frontier Pioneer resolves before Government Incentives. During ACTIONS, optional Banking applies to each performed free action; direct conversion, Ship additions and PLAN passes do not trigger it. Bonus free-action investments are stored separately from used slots. See [the ten-card effects and R01 walkthrough](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.14.md).

The final ten additions are Death Sun, Gravity Drive, Soylent Green, Mercenaries, Filibuster, Insurance, Eminent Domain, Sabotage, Flexible Economy and Foresight. Their controls cover enhanced mixed attacks, carried Ships and ownership of casualties, full-planet replacements, fixed Mercenaries-before-Rapid-Advance order, exact-token lifts, Insurance rewards while own tokens remain, including Ships at an empty home, free SETTLE chains before scoring, Sabotage payment of chosen X without a Research lab discount and inline ongoing powers. Death Sun sacrifices target three Ships OR two Settlements, requiring separate sacrifices for mixed target types. Foresight is private to the active owner in the Status Box; it is excluded from normal simpler Events.

The [0.20 guide](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.20.md) lists the Event test batches and controls; [0.19](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.19.md) records previous presentation changes. Confirmed C01–C04 still govern Gravity Drive, Eminent Domain timing, Soylent eligibility and targetless Sabotage. Remote modes and expansion rules remain later milestones; all 60 supplied card faces/reference texts are prepared.

## Controls and game records

- Use Board, Techs, Event, Actions and Supply, and Player aid, including after scoring. Both aid faces appear together. The top Status Box highlights the current player, followed by the next-step prompt in black. “Players & supply” exposes everyone's public resources/current scores, ordered from the current first player, with invested-slot counts and additional free-action investments shown separately. It starts collapsed at each new required decision, including consecutive turns by the same player, and stays as chosen during draft/view/Credit edits. System status contains numbered top-to-bottom chip order, with 1 at the top; “Since your last turn” defaults to collapsed and lists actions/outcomes since the active player's preceding required decision, including consecutive decisions by that player.
- One-off Event summaries show Effect complete after the entire resolution; ongoing summaries retain their text and selector. Vorsprung choices are sorted high-to-low in Status, require commitment and can also be selected/double-clicked in Techs. Sabotage defaults to the most expensive affordable legal target and matching X. Turtling defaults to up to three available home Ships. Relevant action reminders explain El Mayarah, Economic Boom, Alliance and Black Cloud; Economic Boom shows two invested-slot rockets. Disruptor warns about ineffective opponent-home targets under confirmed Earth First precedence. See [0.21](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.21.md).
- Select a system, then an available action slot. Before Commit, the Status Box lists applicable before, after and chained free-action effects. Investing clears this preview. Adjust the draft, choose Invest immediately above Commit if wanted, and Commit. Attacks allow choosing targets first, increasing the sacrifice to their cost within the available Ships. Production shows linked Ships and Credits vertically, defaults to maximum Ships, and explains Reserve limits. Disruptor-blocked PLAN systems remain inspectable; their placement button is disabled with an explanation, and double-confirmation cannot place there. Empty/impossible actions default to Invest but may still be chosen deliberately.
- SETTLE previews an editable allocation across planets; commitment resolves the allocation, pausing for Explore outcomes and choices. If an opponent's Explore reward displaces an allocated Settlement, only the displaced amount may be reassigned to another eligible planet or kept as Ships. Earlier and queued commitments remain fixed. Non-choice Explore results require Ok. A revealed MOVE INTO is oriented before Ship quantities are chosen; click/tap its system for a clockwise 60-degree preview or use the rotation buttons. Commit is still required; at least one Ship must enter by a legal original route.
- Board system/planet/edge choices select their Status Box control; double-click/double-tap confirms the whole current draft through the same button and warnings. Prophecy lists only face-up Explore effects; Migration retains each player's original total beside the draft total.
- Investments and reinforcement drafts can be allocated through board clicks or quantity controls. Investment edits rebalance other allocations automatically; reinforcements cannot exceed availability. Confirmations guard unused reinforcements, excess attack sacrifices and ineffective Tech purchases. Reserve limit/empty warnings use focused pop-ups. Predictable shortages are checked before commitment with Go back/Confirm; already committed or restored outcomes require Confirm outcome. Flexible economy returns and waiting-effect controls remain in the Status Box, outside the warning. Committed interrupts without Go back omit the intended-outcome question. Empty non-Invested MOVE INTO/SETTLE/ATTACK and inbound black-hole losses also require confirmation. Cancelling a movement follow-up preserves its already committed reveal/orientation.
- Flexible economy owners have the matching subheading and one-Credit return button below Status Box resources. Foresight controls remain above them, with their heading above the button. Reserve shortfall pop-ups have only warning/reminder and Go back/Confirm outcome: no adjustment controls. For predictable shortages, Go back preserves the uncommitted draft. After a committed reveal, it returns to Status controls for the unfinished effect while keeping all earlier gains, reveals and placements fixed. Return Credits there, then Apply effect; production/reinforcement quantities also live outside the interrupt. An accepted shortfall applies the available amount and leaves any remainder uncompleted. Only active-player shortages interrupt. Rival Explore gifts use available Reserve and fail automatically if it is empty, with no refill/confirmation handoff even for Flexible economy owners. The limit/empty dialogs confirm reduced results; shortfall also supports returning to the Status Box to refill and resume, so they retain their distinct controls.
- Drag the map background or pan with buttons. Touch supports one-finger pan and two-finger pinch around the gesture focus. Use the grouped zoom/Reset View controls as alternatives. Swipe left/right over the view navigation to move between views; gestures on the board never switch views.
- Completion and resumed completed games scroll/focus Expedition results once, below the sticky header. Later view selection remains free.
- B/T/E/A/P open the five views and scroll above their panel, retaining about 2cm more Status Box context; S scrolls to the Status Box at the top of the document. Desktop headings show their key in brackets; phones omit these hints. With map focus, arrows pan, +/− zoom, and R resets the view. Escape closes Help/card details. ? opens Help. Character shortcuts can be disabled; editable fields and browser modifiers are respected. Tabs also support arrow/Home/End navigation.
- Live interface text has a 10pt minimum, including board labels at reduced zoom. Printed card/aid artwork retains its source lettering and has readable semantic reference text. All chips remain visible in compact owner-coloured stacks, with opaque legal-chip rings behind them: yellow blinks slowly, blue hover/focus stays steady, and reduced motion disables blinking; Settlement rockets are twice their previous size.
- The phase guide appears by default, can be hidden, and can be replayed from Help. This is the initial contextual teaching layer; per-seat progress and teaching of later multiplayer features remain outstanding under [TUTORIAL.md](TUTORIAL.md).
- Accepted commands autosave on this device, including pending Explore, acknowledgements, redistribution, orientation, movement, acquisition, free actions, Rapid conversions, carried-Ship movement, replacements, Filibuster, Insurance, fixed after-MOVE order, Mercenaries and the endgame queue. Blocked storage leaves a usable session with a visible status. Version 0.8/0.9/0.10/0.11/0.14/0.15/0.16/0.17/0.18/0.19/0.20 engine records are upgraded by replay, handling new pauses and automatic handover while preserving their outcomes, including the old max-Credit production default where applicable. An incompatible/corrupt save reports failure and permits a new game.
- “Game record” downloads JSON containing application version, timestamp, seed, players, ordered commands, current state, round/event configuration, completion status through `phase`, and final scores/winners when finished. The command history can reproduce the final state with the same engine/content version using `replayGame`, including recorded historical-rule boundaries. ## Architecture and assets

The TypeScript engine accepts an actor, command and expected revision, clones state, validates the choice, resolves it, records the command, and checks supply invariants. Rejected commands leave the original unchanged. The UI keeps draft choices outside accepted game state. Board rendering draws every hex background first, all component groups next and names last, so neighbouring artwork cannot cover tokens. The same generated parts serve Tech previews. This provides a testable foundation for later server authority; a local revision check is not remote authentication.

Application/rules engine 1.0.0 uses the unchanged schema 11 and content identifier `event-decks-board-targets-8`. `initialEventCards` records ten cards for new two/three-player games and eight for new four-to-seven-player games, including test batches. Older ten-card test saves retain their initial size when replayed. Setup variables select the ten-card display, standard/R01 scenario and full/simpler/legacy Foresight-test Event mode and independent Event batch; serializable parent action contexts preserve interrupted nested chains. State is plain versioned JSON. Confirmations reuse a validated proposed state only while their original game remains current, avoiding duplicate command calculation without bypassing stale-state checks. Stable system/card IDs link rules to [layout data](../src/layouts-data.json), [card text](../src/cards-data.json), and the [art manifest](../src/art-data.json). Artwork can be replaced without changing engine IDs. Board positions use axial coordinates with pointy-top geometry; planets, 142 printed settlement indicators and chip snap positions use normalised source-image/vector anchors and rotate with their system. Fleet badges and Planet Nine use a search for empty space to minimise overlap. Tech system previews reuse the board renderer and shared SVG definitions, preserve hidden backs and show current public pieces in compact 96×110px graphics. Planet choices size to content in a horizontal row. Unchanged target lists and preview markup are cached; replacements invalidate the caches. Chip rims have thin side outlines with an unoutlined centre. Read-only orientation/asteroid queries share unchanged state and isolate only their temporary system edit; accepted commands still clone and validate a complete independent state. Connectivity precomputes fixed edges/warps once per query. Artwork placement avoids per-candidate arrays while preserving the exact selected coordinates. Package/export/save identity is centralised in version metadata, and TypeScript rejects unused code.

The 85 WebP assets total approximately 1.51 MB: 14 system fronts, 60 card faces, two aid faces, three backs, one cover, the first-player token, the Planet Nine token, circular black-hole Explore image, and the additional asteroid-belt/warp tokens. Printed elongated holes are omitted from the Clarke/Lem/Adams export layers; all hole types use the same circular image at their semantic positions. Detailed card/aid images load on demand. The cover comes from the matching embedded rulebook illustration; no claim is made that the separately supplied PNG binary has been archived. The seven current silhouettes are designer-approved and the STL handoff is closed, with distinct dot, stripe, bar, chevron, checker, wave and triangle chip rims.

To regenerate the PDF-derived assets, install PyMuPDF and Pillow outside the application dependency set and run:

```sh
python scripts/prepare-assets.py --source-dir /path/to/supplied/pdfs
```

The script reads the source catalogue and validates extracted planet capacities/features. It never runs TTS Lua or depends on live Steam URLs. Le Guin has four planets of capacity 2/2/1/1 and no default black hole; Lem has both left and right printed asteroid belts. These correct earlier catalogue transcriptions; the designer's supplied source versions remain current. The removed “◉ +1” label represented an additional black hole, now drawn directly.

Additional frozen-state checks match legal targets and exact artwork geometry captured from engine 0.22 across all six player counts; a genuine 0.22 sealed-auction checkpoint resumes without losing private bids.

`npm run build` performs strict TypeScript compilation, builds the static application, verifies every artwork reference, checks relative asset paths, and enforces the 200 KB compressed JavaScript budget. The initial build is around 77 KB compressed JavaScript plus 10 KB CSS and a 54 KB cover, around 141 KB combined before HTTP headers. This is a build-size measurement, not a measured page-load or interaction-time result. GitHub Actions repeats the checks and uploads the static build plus a portable desktop playtest copy. The portable copy inlines the bundled module/CSS and keeps the art folder alongside `index.html`; local-file behaviour still needs browser verification.

Visual browser automation was unavailable in the implementation environment. The designer has tested on desktop, including a narrow window. No hosted deployment, formal cross-browser visual pass, real-phone hardware pass, Core Web Vitals result, or accessibility audit is claimed. The iPhone Files preview is not a Safari playtest; follow [PHONE_TESTING.md](PHONE_TESTING.md). The immediate review is a complete desktop game and a phone game, including pinch/pan/swipe separation, small-screen layout, colour/shape/rim readability, keyboard and browser zoom, and pending-choice refresh.

## Remaining product work

The prototype is trusted-device pass-and-play: the browser contains the full hidden state, anyone sharing it controls the current seat, and optional local storage is its only persistence. It has no accounts, networking, room list, chat, user-email collection, private owner area, or remote security boundary.

Follow [the next-phase plan](NEXT_PHASE.md): add authoritative rooms/seats, public/private lobby, independent clients, projected hidden information, command deduplication/recovery and the confirmed dropout policy. Add asynchronous play with its own timing/removal policy as specified in [ASYNCHRONOUS_PLAY.md](ASYNCHRONOUS_PLAY.md). Add simultaneous actions, with the four-plus-player recommendation. Owner administration, durable game/result history, feedback, consented mailing/export, per-player tutorial, final assets and production verification remain tracked in [REMINDERS.md](REMINDERS.md).

Apple iOS/iPadOS and Android apps are required future deliverables, specified in [MOBILE_APPS.md](MOBILE_APPS.md). They share the browser client/rules and online service; app packages and store delivery are not part of this local milestone.
