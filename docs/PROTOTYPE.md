17 November 2026. This document describes the implemented build; the project brief retains the full product requirements.

Detailed implementation changes: [fixed Tech order, corrected Insurance and simpler Reserve warnings](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.17.md); previous [0.16](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.16.md), [0.15](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.15.md), [0.14](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.14.md), [0.13](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.13.md), [0.12](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.12.md), [0.11](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.11.md), [0.10](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.10.md) and [0.9](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.9.md) iterations.

## Playable scope

Two to seven people share one browser/device. Normal turns, selectable ten-Tech displays and Events used as a round tracker form a complete game. All thirty Tech effects work. Last ten Techs is the default display; its explicit nonstandard Foresight test option keeps that card while Event effects/auctions remain disabled. Normal simpler Events exclude Foresight and replace it in presets with a seeded random legal value-2/3 Tech under R08. All five printed presets, the earlier playtest displays and Random are available. Simultaneous turns are not offered. The first seat starts; seat order is entered at setup. A map code seeds home selection, hidden systems, Explore tokens, and the Event deck reproducibly.

Each player chooses a home by button or double-click/double-tap, receiving three Settlements and three Ships. The optional R01 designer scenario starts every player with the five chain Techs, accounting for their markers from Reserve; it is saved/exported as a setup variable and scores those Techs normally. This permits immediate testing at all player counts, since four-plus-player normal games cannot acquire all five before their final ACTIONS phase. PLAN places four chips each in order, with a changeable preview followed by “Place chip here” or double-click/double-tap confirmation. ACTIONS skips players with no available top chip; each player resolves their four chips into unused action slots, acting or Investing. Economy cashes Investments on the Board, draws a pair of round-tracker Events with an explicit review/continue step, permits one Tech purchase each, reinforces on the Board, and automatically passes the first-player token before the next round. Two/three-player games last six rounds; four to seven last five. The Economy after the last pair has been consumed still permits Tech purchases, then scores without reinforcement.

All ten Family Techs work: Agility, Artificial Warp, Assembly Line, Banking, Disruptor, Genesis, Kessler Syndrome, Patents, Planet Killer, and Planet Nine. Optional acquisition placements can be declined; Genesis and Disruptor resolve their required legal choices. The five-player setup consumes the spare asteroid token. Explore-token black holes have a separate supply from Planet Killer's feature token. An area enclosed by asteroid belts is legal if warps still connect it.

Ten more Techs now work: Population Explosion, Government Incentives, Frontier Pioneer, Rapid Advance, Spice, Arcologies, Cloaking, Patrol, Research Lab and Strategic Stall. Free actions resolve nested before/body/after effects while retaining the root turn, token and slot. Rapid Advance follows the almanac and is not SETTLE; Spice cannot trigger itself and recomputes its second output after the first chain. Frontier Pioneer resolves before Government Incentives. During ACTIONS, optional Banking applies to each performed free action; direct conversion, Ship additions and PLAN passes do not trigger it. Bonus free-action investments are stored separately from used slots. See [the ten-card effects and R01 walkthrough](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.14.md).

The final ten additions are Death Sun, Gravity Drive, Soylent Green, Mercenaries, Filibuster, Insurance, Eminent Domain, Sabotage, Flexible Economy and Foresight. Their controls cover enhanced mixed attacks, carried Ships and ownership of casualties, full-planet replacements, fixed Mercenaries-before-Rapid-Advance order, exact-token lifts, Insurance rewards while own tokens remain, including Ships at an empty home, free SETTLE chains before scoring, Sabotage payment of chosen X without a Research lab discount and inline ongoing powers. Death Sun sacrifices target three Ships OR two Settlements, requiring separate sacrifices for mixed target types. Foresight is private to the active owner in the Status Box; it is excluded from normal simpler Events.

The [0.17 guide](PLAYTEST_CHANGES_0.17.md) lists the updated effects and designer-confirmed C01–C04 rulings for Gravity Drive, Eminent Domain timing, Soylent eligibility and targetless Sabotage. All Event effects/auctions, remote modes and expansion rules remain later milestones; all 60 supplied card faces/reference texts are prepared.

## Controls and game records

- Use Board, Techs, Event, Actions and Supply, and Player aid, including after scoring. Both aid faces appear together. The top Status Box highlights the current player, followed by the next-step prompt in black. “Players & supply” exposes everyone's public resources/current scores, ordered from the current first player. System status contains numbered top-to-bottom chip order, with 1 at the top; “Since your last turn” defaults to collapsed and lists actions/outcomes since the active player's preceding required decision, including consecutive decisions by that player.
- Select a system, then an available action slot. Before Commit, the Status Box lists applicable before, after and chained free-action effects. Investing clears this preview. Adjust the draft, choose Invest immediately above Commit if wanted, and Commit. Attacks allow choosing targets first, increasing the sacrifice to their cost within the available Ships. Production shows linked Ships and Credits vertically, defaults to maximum Ships, and explains Reserve limits. Disruptor-blocked PLAN systems remain inspectable; their placement button is disabled with an explanation, and double-confirmation cannot place there. Empty/impossible actions default to Invest but may still be chosen deliberately.
- SETTLE previews an editable allocation across planets; commitment resolves the allocation, pausing for Explore outcomes and choices. If an opponent's Explore reward displaces an allocated Settlement, only the displaced amount may be reassigned to another eligible planet or kept as Ships. Earlier and queued commitments remain fixed. Non-choice Explore results require Ok. A revealed MOVE INTO is oriented before Ship quantities are chosen; at least one Ship must enter by a legal original route.
- Investments and reinforcement drafts can be allocated through board clicks or quantity controls. Investment edits rebalance other allocations automatically; reinforcements cannot exceed availability. Confirmations guard unused reinforcements, excess attack sacrifices and ineffective Tech purchases. Reserve limit/empty warnings use focused pop-ups. Predictable shortages are checked before commitment with Go back/Confirm; already committed or restored outcomes require Confirm outcome. Flexible economy returns and waiting-effect controls remain in the Status Box, outside the warning. Committed interrupts without Go back omit the intended-outcome question. Empty non-Invested MOVE INTO/SETTLE/ATTACK and inbound black-hole losses also require confirmation. Cancelling a movement follow-up preserves its already committed reveal/orientation.
- Flexible economy owners have the matching subheading and one-Credit return button below Status Box resources. Foresight controls remain above them. Reserve shortfall pop-ups have only warning/reminder and Go back/Confirm outcome: no adjustment controls. For predictable shortages, Go back preserves the uncommitted draft. After a committed reveal, it returns to Status controls for the unfinished effect while keeping all earlier gains, reveals and placements fixed. Return Credits there, then Apply effect; production/reinforcement quantities also live outside the interrupt. An accepted shortfall applies the available amount and leaves any remainder uncompleted. Foreign Explore gifts use their recipient's resources without advancing the acting turn.
- Drag the map background or pan with buttons. Touch supports one-finger pan and two-finger pinch around the gesture focus. Use the grouped zoom/Reset View controls as alternatives. Swipe left/right over the view navigation to move between views; gestures on the board never switch views.
- Completion and resumed completed games scroll/focus Expedition results once, below the sticky header. Later view selection remains free.
- B/T/E/A/P open the five views and scroll above their panel, retaining about 2cm more Status Box context; S scrolls to the Status Box at the top of the document. Desktop headings show their key in brackets; phones omit these hints. With map focus, arrows pan, +/− zoom, and R resets the view. Escape closes Help/card details. ? opens Help. Character shortcuts can be disabled; editable fields and browser modifiers are respected. Tabs also support arrow/Home/End navigation.
- Live interface text has a 10pt minimum, including board labels at reduced zoom. Printed card/aid artwork retains its source lettering and has readable semantic reference text. All chips remain visible in compact owner-coloured stacks, with opaque legal-chip rings behind them: yellow blinks slowly, blue hover/focus stays steady, and reduced motion disables blinking; Settlement rockets are twice their previous size.
- The phase guide appears by default, can be hidden, and can be replayed from Help. This is the initial contextual teaching layer; per-seat progress and teaching of later multiplayer features remain outstanding under [TUTORIAL.md](TUTORIAL.md).
- Accepted commands autosave on this device, including pending Explore, acknowledgements, redistribution, orientation, movement, acquisition, free actions, Rapid conversions, carried-Ship movement, replacements, Filibuster, Insurance, fixed after-MOVE order, Mercenaries and the endgame queue. Blocked storage leaves a usable session with a visible status. Version 0.8/0.9/0.10/0.11/0.14/0.15/0.16 engine records are upgraded by replay, handling new pauses and automatic handover while preserving their outcomes, including the old max-Credit production default where applicable. An incompatible/corrupt save reports failure and permits a new game.
- “Game record” downloads JSON containing application version, timestamp, seed, players, ordered commands, current state, round/event configuration, completion status through `phase`, and final scores/winners when finished. The command history can reproduce the final state with the same engine/content version using `replayGame`, including recorded historical-rule boundaries. Historical 0.15/0.16 combat, payment and Insurance results are preserved; new commands use the corrected rules. An unfinished 0.15 Reserve shortfall upgrades to the new recovery continuation. Exporting does not create a private backend record.

## Architecture and assets

The TypeScript engine accepts an actor, command and expected revision, clones state, validates the choice, resolves it, records the command, and checks supply invariants. Rejected commands leave the original unchanged. The UI keeps draft choices outside accepted game state. This provides a testable foundation for later server authority; a local revision check is not remote authentication.

Application/rules engine 0.17.0 uses schema 8 and content identifier `ordered-techs-shortfall-review-5`. Setup variables select the ten-card display, standard/R01 scenario and normal simpler/nonstandard Foresight-test Event mode; serializable parent action contexts preserve interrupted nested chains. State is plain versioned JSON. Confirmations reuse a validated proposed state only while their original game remains current, avoiding duplicate command calculation without bypassing stale-state checks. Stable system/card IDs link rules to [layout data](../src/layouts-data.json), [card text](../src/cards-data.json), and the [art manifest](../src/art-data.json). Artwork can be replaced without changing engine IDs. Board positions use axial coordinates with pointy-top geometry; planets, 142 printed settlement indicators and chip snap positions use normalised source-image/vector anchors and rotate with their system. Fleet badges and Planet Nine use a search for empty space to minimise overlap. Tech system previews reuse the board renderer and shared SVG definitions, preserve hidden backs and show current public pieces in compact 96×110px graphics. Planet choices size to content in a horizontal row. Unchanged target lists and preview markup are cached; replacements invalidate the caches. Chip rims have thin side outlines with an unoutlined centre.

The 85 WebP assets total approximately 1.51 MB: 14 system fronts, 60 card faces, two aid faces, three backs, one cover, the first-player token, the Planet Nine token, circular black-hole Explore image, and the additional asteroid-belt/warp tokens. Printed elongated holes are omitted from the Clarke/Lem/Adams export layers; all hole types use the same circular image at their semantic positions. Detailed card/aid images load on demand. The cover comes from the matching embedded rulebook illustration; no claim is made that the separately supplied PNG binary has been archived. Seven silhouettes remain provisional pending STLs, with distinct dot, stripe, bar, chevron, checker, wave and triangle chip rims.

To regenerate the PDF-derived assets, install PyMuPDF and Pillow outside the application dependency set and run:

```sh
python scripts/prepare-assets.py --source-dir /path/to/supplied/pdfs
```

The script reads the source catalogue and validates extracted planet capacities/features. It never runs TTS Lua or depends on live Steam URLs. Le Guin has four planets of capacity 2/2/1/1 and no default black hole; Lem has both left and right printed asteroid belts. These correct earlier catalogue transcriptions; the designer's supplied source versions remain current. The removed “◉ +1” label represented an additional black hole, now drawn directly.

`npm test` passes 205 tests covering source/setup counts and all player layouts, action stacks and slots, revisions, hidden reveal checkpoints, movement/asteroids/warps/black holes, Explore gifts and supply limits, production, attack insurance, Investments, reinforcement, all thirty implemented Techs, the exact R01 sequence and non-recursion, before-effect quantity selection, end timing, scoring and ties. 126 seeded full games (72 Family games, 18 New Techs/mixed/scenario games and 36 last-ten/printed/random games across all six player counts) check the forty-rocket invariant after every accepted command. A complete seven-player record is replayed to the identical final state. Asset checks verify all 85 files, printed settlement anchors and feature geometry. Genuine 0.8/0.9/0.10/0.11/0.14/0.15/0.16 engine saves migrate by replay, including pending reveals and matching completed scores/winners. JSDOM tests exercise setup/preset/scenario selection, draft/confirm PLAN including blocked-system inspection, the full free-action chain and refresh, Research Lab prices, Strategic Stall, Arcologies anchors, target-first combat, allocation balancing/limits, acknowledgements, locked Settlement redistribution, decision history, shortcuts/scrolling, selected-first choosers, minimum text, gestures, storage failures, escaping, Tech-effect previews, mixed Death Sun costs, Gravity casualties, Soylent replacements, endgame controls/results scrolling, inline private powers, Reserve recovery through nested effects and home double-click/double-tap, and all five final-state views.

`npm run build` performs strict TypeScript compilation, builds the static application, verifies every artwork reference, checks relative asset paths, and enforces the 200 KB compressed JavaScript budget. The initial build is around 64 KB compressed JavaScript plus 9 KB CSS and a 54 KB cover, around 127 KB combined before HTTP headers. This is a build-size measurement, not a measured page-load or interaction-time result. GitHub Actions repeats the checks and uploads the static build plus a portable desktop playtest copy. The portable copy inlines the bundled module/CSS and keeps the art folder alongside `index.html`; local-file behaviour still needs browser verification.

Visual browser automation was unavailable in the implementation environment. The designer has tested on desktop, including a narrow window. No hosted deployment, formal cross-browser visual pass, real-phone hardware pass, Core Web Vitals result, or accessibility audit is claimed. The iPhone Files preview is not a Safari playtest; follow [PHONE_TESTING.md](PHONE_TESTING.md). The immediate review is a complete desktop game and a phone game, including pinch/pan/swipe separation, small-screen layout, colour/shape/rim readability, keyboard and browser zoom, and pending-choice refresh.

## Remaining product work

The prototype is trusted-device pass-and-play: the browser contains the full hidden state, anyone sharing it controls the current seat, and optional local storage is its only persistence. It has no accounts, networking, room list, chat, user-email collection, private owner area, or remote security boundary.

Next add authoritative rooms/seats, public/private lobby, independent clients, projected hidden information, command deduplication/recovery and the confirmed dropout policy. Add asynchronous play with its own timing/removal policy as specified in [ASYNCHRONOUS_PLAY.md](ASYNCHRONOUS_PLAY.md).
